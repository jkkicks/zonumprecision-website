How it works

One email thread, from the first file to the shipment.

  1. You send files

    Use the form or email me. Attach the STEP file and PDF drawing, and add quantity, material, finish and the date you need parts if you know them.

  2. I review and quote

    I check manufacturability on the same thread and send a quote with a lead time for the job. You approve the quote, then I schedule the cut.

  3. I machine and inspect

    I program, fixture and run the job, then check the critical features against your drawing.

  4. I ship, you revise

    Parts leave deburred and packed. When the design changes, send the new files. Saved setups and programs make the next quote and cut faster where they still fit.

What I check first on a messy repo

  1. API keys. Any key found in the repo or git history gets rotated and moved to environment variables.
  2. Logins and permissions. I check every route, including the admin pages nobody linked.
  3. Duplication. The same fetch wrapper pasted in four places becomes one function.
  4. Tests. I add them where a regression costs money: checkout, payments, sign-in.
  5. Silent failures. Errors that were being swallowed get logged or surfaced.

You get the fixes, tests that run automatically on every change, and a short doc on how it works and what is still worth doing.

Questions before you send files

Something else? Email me.

What should I send for a quote?

A STEP file and a PDF drawing with tolerances and callouts, plus quantity, material, finish and the date you need parts. Fusion F3D or DXF is fine if that is what you have. With only a model, send it and I will ask which features matter. The form takes up to 6 files, 20 MB each, 40 MB total. Larger files can go to jacob@zonumprecision.com.

How fast will I hear back, and how long will parts take?

I reply to every request, usually within a day or two, with a quote and a lead time for that job. The lead time depends on the job, so each quote states its own.

Do you take one-offs?

Yes. A quantity of one is normal.

What materials and finishes do you offer?

6061 and 7075 aluminum, steels, stainless, brass, Delrin / acetal and UHMW. Edges are broken here. Anodize and bead blast go to outside shops I have used on my own jobs. Something else? Ask.

What tolerances can you hold?

Tolerance depends on the feature, material and size. The quote lists the callouts I can hold and any I would change.

Should I order through Subtract or directly?

Either works. Use Subtract if your company already buys there.

Can you sign an NDA?

Yes, for parts or software. Send it before you share files and I will review it first.

How is software work priced, and what do you need to quote it?

Usually a fixed-scope audit first. Cleanup and build work is then quoted per project with clear deliverables. To quote, I need a repo link or a short description of the stack, what is broken or risky, and any deadline.

Can you take over a repo I built with an AI tool?

Yes. I audit it first, fix the auth and security problems I find, and write a handoff doc.
